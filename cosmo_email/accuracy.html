Which Phantom examples run on the GPU, and how closely they agree

Phantom’s build system defines 136 setups. Leaving out those that need physics the GPU path does not yet have (MHD, dust, radiation, self-gravity, general relativity, adaptive particle refinement), the test bundles and the obsolete ones leaves 36. Each of those was built for the GPU and run twice from the same starting point: once on the GPU path, once on Phantom’s normal CPU path, from the same binary. This page groups the results by how well the two agree, and lists the targeted tests behind the new features.

How agreement is measured

One step

Take a single step from the same state on both paths. How differently do the particles’ velocities change, as a fraction of how much they change? 10⁻⁹ means agreement to nine digits. About 10⁻⁸ is as close as the density solve’s own tolerance allows.

Energy drift

Over 200 steps, how much does total energy drift on the GPU compared with the CPU? 1.00 means the same drift.

Divergence, R

Over 200 steps, how far do GPU and CPU drift apart, compared with two CPU runs that differ by a tiny change in settings? Around 1 or below means the GPU is no further from the CPU than the CPU is from itself. R is measured at each example’s own h tolerance; where it came out above 1, the runs were repeated with h converged to 10⁻⁸ (second R column).

Agree to rounding once h is fully convergedmatches CPU

Single steps agree to seven or eight digits and energy drifts identically. At the example’s own smoothing-length tolerance (10⁻⁴ for most) R is 3–10: the two paths stop the h iteration at slightly different points within that tolerance, which sets a small offset in the first steps. With the long runs repeated with h converged to 10⁻⁸, the offset goes and GPU and CPU stay 30 to more than 100 times closer than the reference pair.

Limited by precision, not by the GPUprecision

On a perfectly regular lattice (taylorgreen, wave) the forces on each particle almost cancel, so tiny rounding differences dominate. Phantom’s CPU path stores one factor in the force sum in single precision; the GPU keeps it in double, so the difference reflects the CPU’s precision. blob is a single-precision build, which limits the one-step comparison; over 200 steps it agrees well.

Targeted tests

Tests written for specific features, beyond running the examples.

Periodic boundaries

A small periodic Sedov blast run past the point where the shock wraps through the box faces, GPU against CPU. Particles near the faces agree as well as those in the middle.

energy agreement 2.1×10⁻⁹ · 148 particles crossed the boundary

Quintic kernel

Sedov and a torus built with the M6 quintic spline, GPU against CPU. The same agreement as the cubic kernel; cubic results are unchanged bit for bit.

Sedov energy 9.5×10⁻⁹ · torus kinetic energy 2.3×10⁻⁹ at t = 1

A particle that neighbours everything

A torus in which one extra particle is switched on far from the rest, so its smoothing length grows until every other particle is its neighbour. The GPU path does the full sum without truncating it; the step is slower, because the octree’s smallest cells fill up.

Regression checks for every change

Each change was checked against the one before on a Sedov blast (174,000 particles, 172 steps) and a torus (52,805 particles, 1050 steps). Changes meant to be exact were bit-identical; the speed-ups changed results only at the rounding level, with agreement against the CPU unchanged.

Sedov energy 8.5×10⁻⁹ · torus energy 1.6×10⁻⁴ over 1050 steps

Open questions

  • exoALMA. Agrees to 6×10⁻¹⁰ in one step, but over 200 steps its total energy differs from the CPU run by 4×10⁻³, with h converged or not. Not yet traced.
  • kh. Still separates (R ≈ 4) with h converged; likely the CPU’s single-precision factor on its starting lattice, as for taylorgreen and wave, but not confirmed.
  • Boundary particles (the shock tube, isowind) are refused rather than supported. Supporting them needs the GPU to hold fixed particles apart from evolving ones.
  • HII regions (h2region) need Phantom’s kd-tree, which the GPU path replaces.
  • Which code: the examples ran on the periodic-boundary code, with cooling and the heating switches added for adiabaticdisc, grdisc and blob. The later speed-ups were checked on Sedov, torus, periodic Sedov and both discs, and change results only at the rounding level.
