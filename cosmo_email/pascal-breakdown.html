Sedov 1.4M on a Quadro Pascal: where a step goes

The same benchmark, the same code and the same protocol as the datacentre runs, on a card with roughly 1/32 FP64 throughput. The interesting result is not the speed — it is that almost everything except the arithmetic disappears.

Where a step goes

Shares of a step, so the shape can be read across four very different machines at once. The step times differ by ninety-fold, so this is deliberately not to scale; the datacentre page has the three fast platforms on an absolute millisecond scale.

All four follow the same protocol: 1,368,000 particles, both CFL factors 10⁻³ so a step is one density solve and one force pass, median of five rounds. The Pascal run times 120 steps from a 20-step restart rather than differencing steps 21–120 of a 120-step run, but its start-up is 0.3 s of 395.7 s, so the rates are comparable. On the CPU there are no transfers and the neighbour searches sit inside the density and force rows.

What it says

  • The two neighbour kernels are 95% of the density solve, and the force kernel is 96% of the force pass. On GH200 the same figures are 73% and 90%, on A100 65% and 79%.
  • Transfers are 3.4% of the step, against 5.8% on GH200 and 14.4% on A100 — the slower the arithmetic, the less the PCIe link matters.
  • The octree build is 0.1%. On the CPU path, building the kd-tree is 40.7% of a step and is the single largest item.
  • Host-side work is 0.5%, against 34.5% on A100. The staging, ordering and cons2prim that dominate the fast platforms are invisible here.

A card whose FP64 rate is roughly 1/32 of its FP32 rate turns this into an almost pure arithmetic benchmark, and the result is that the method is kernel-bound rather than bandwidth- or tree-bound. That is easy to assert where the kernels are half the step; it is hard to argue with when they are 95% of it. The corollary matters more for what to port next: every part of the infrastructure — tree, lists, transfers, host staging — scales with things a weak GPU barely punishes, so the cost of adding physics is bounded by FP64 throughput and little else.

Reading the rate from a log

0.415 ×10⁶ particles/s: 395.7 s of wall over 120 steps, median of five rounds that spanned 0.43 s. That is 33× behind the A100 and 90× behind GH200, and 11× behind a 120-core EPYC — about what a 1/32-rate FP64 part should give.

Take the rate from Total wall time and Since code start: N timesteps. The step row of the timing table covers one dump interval; it happens to be the whole run here because there is only one, but that is not generally true.

The timer fix matters for this measurement: Phantom's step timer is real*4 seconds since the start of the month, which quantises to 1/8 s from the 13th onward.
