Sedov 1.4M on a Quadro Pascal: where a step goes

The same code as the datacentre runs, on a card with roughly 1/32 FP64 throughput. The interesting result is not the speed — it is that almost everything except the arithmetic disappears.

The two bars are not the same measurement and the rates are not comparable: the GH200 run follows the Shamrock protocol (both CFL factors 10⁻³, one force pass per step, steps 21–120 of an evolved dump), while these runs use Phantom's default CFL and integrate from the initial lattice to t = 0.1. Shares of a step are still worth putting side by side; a rate is not.

What it says

  • The two neighbour kernels are 94.5% of the density solve, and the force kernel is 96.4% of the force pass. On GH200 the same two figures are 73% and 90%.
  • Transfers are 3.0% of the step, against 5.8% on GH200 — and all the host-side staging and ordering machinery lands inside the 4.4%, where on GH200 the host side is 22.2%.
  • The octree build is 0.1%. On the CPU path, building the kd-tree is 40.7% of a step.

A card whose FP64 rate is roughly 1/32 of its FP32 rate turns this into an almost pure arithmetic benchmark, and the result is that the method is kernel-bound rather than bandwidth- or tree-bound. That is easy to assert on hardware where the kernels are half the step; it is hard to argue with when they are 95% of it. Run on unsuitable hardware, failing in exactly the predicted way, is evidence rather than a wasted afternoon.

Reading the rate from a log

These runs give 0.34 ×10⁶ particles/s: total wall 1431 s over 359 steps.

Take it from Total wall time and Since code start: N timesteps, not from the timing table. The step row in that table covers one dump interval, not the run — the last one in these logs reads 39.22 s against a 1431 s run.

For a figure comparable with the benchmark table, the protocol needs both CFL factors at 10⁻³, a restart dump evolved five steps, and the step row differenced between nmax = 120 and nmax = 20, median of five rounds. It also needs the timer fix: Phantom's step timer is real*4 seconds since the start of the month, which quantises to 1/8 s from the 13th onward, and the protocol subtracts two such numbers.
