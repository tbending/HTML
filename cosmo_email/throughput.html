Phantom on GPU against Shamrock and Phantom on CPU

Two benchmarks: the time-stepping test from the Shamrock paper, and Shamrock’s own single-GPU benchmark at 2²⁵ particles. Rows marked Shamrock are figures Shamrock published; the rest are our measurements.

Measured rows. One export of phantom 1f683c9db with cosmoSPHere d6ca77b, built identically on every platform, 16 September 2026. The commits going into the pull requests, phantom d5b51c873 and cosmoSPHere 999fe37, give bit-identical results. Median of five rounds, range beneath. Each round differences runs of 20 and 120 steps, so the rate covers steps 21–120 and excludes start-up and I/O.

Protocol as in the paper. Phantom’s Sedov setup in its default periodic box (npartx = 100, close-packed, 1,368,000 particles, the third point of the paper’s Fig. 17), evolved five steps to make the restart every run starts from; IND_TIMESTEPS=no, both CFL factors 10⁻³. The restart was made once, on the CPU, and shared by all platforms.

Paper figures are read from the text and Fig. 17 (“≳ 2×10⁶” and “~12×10⁶”). The paper’s A100 is the SXM4 (NVLink) card; ours is the PCIe card, in a host whose other cores were busy with unrelated single-threaded jobs, so ours were bound to 16 idle cores beside the GPU. On 64 cores the EPYC 9745 gives 3.94×10⁶. Our code is likely to perform better on an A100 node with a faster link.

Builds use -O3 with phantom’s standard gfortran flags. Phantom’s timer start time is kept in double precision for these runs; unpatched, section timers are quantised to 1/8 s mid-month. This changes no physics.

Same physics. The same Sedov blast at scale: periodic close-packed box, M4 kernel, adiabatic γ = 5/3, viscosity switch α 0–1, αu = 1, β = 2, zero background energy and a kernel-weighted injection at the centre. Shamrock caps the count at 2²⁵; phantom’s setup at npartx = 288 gives 33,561,216 particles.

Timing. Shamrock divides N by the fastest of ten replayed steps with dt = 0. Phantom cannot replay a step, so we ran from the setup dump to nmax = 2, 3, …, 12 and differenced consecutive runs, timing each of steps 3–12, twice. The quoted rate is the median of those 20 steps; the fastest single step gives 55.0×10⁶, though phantom’s timer resolves only 10 ms of the 0.66 s step. Both CFL factors 10⁻³, so each step is one density solve and one force pass, as a replayed step is. With Shamrock’s CFL 0.1 phantom’s corrector adds a second force pass (33.4×10⁶); a dt = 0 replay can never trigger that.

Other published results on this benchmark (Shamrock documentation): H200 NVL (AdaptiveCpp) 14.5×10⁶, Intel Data Center GPU Max 1550 (DPC++) 14.6×10⁶. Same binary as the table above, 17 September 2026; peak GPU memory 12.6 GB.
